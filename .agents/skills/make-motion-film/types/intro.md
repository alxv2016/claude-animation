# Intro

A short opener, 5–15 s: who someone is, or a brand's sting before other content. One idea, said
fast, ending on a name or mark.

## Inputs

| Input       | Required | Default                                             |
| ----------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Who or what | Yes      | The model itself when the prompt says "who you are" |
| Length      | No       | 10 s                                                |
| Brand       | No       | Studio default; capture-brand for a company         |
| Format      | No       | 16:9                                                |

## Structure

| Part  | Share | On screen                                                              |
| ----- | ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook  | 0–2 s | Motion from the first frame; a word or a shape, not a logo yet         |
| Claim | ~50 % | Two or three facts or traits, one per bar, each with its own technique |
| Name  | 2–3 s | The name or mark resolving; it holds still for at least a second       |

- **One idea**: an intro is not a short showreel; pick one through-line (a shape that becomes the
  mark, a word that types into the name).
- **The name holds**: the last 1–2 s are still enough to read and remember.

## Sound

Effects that resolve on the name: a riser into an impact with a picture hit on the mark. Music only
from the user's track (R13).

## Checks

The anti-slop guardrails are studio rules already: no text in the corners, no frame borders (R7),
no fade-ins (R6), no glow (R8). Check them first in an intro; there are few frames to hide them in.

## Example prompt

Kept verbatim; this file is its home. Pattern from @1littlecoder.

```text
make a dynamic 10-second motion graphics video that introduces who you are as Opus 5.5.
Avoid frames and text in the corners, the usual giveaways of AI-made video.
```
